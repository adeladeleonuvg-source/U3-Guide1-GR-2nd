In this interactive whiteboard guide based on Our World 2, we will master how to talk about feelings, greet friends, describe people's expressions, and count things using regular and irregular plurals. Let's learn step by step!

1

How are you? & I'm + Adjective

We use this structure to ask and answer about our feelings and physical state right now.

Questions & Answers

  • • How are you?
  • → I'm great! / I'm fine. / I'm OK. / I'm tired.
  • Note: "I'm" = "I am" | "We're" = "We are"

He / She Looks + Adjective

  • • Describing others' expressions:
  • → She looks hungry. / He looks angry.
  • → You look surprised. / He looks excited.
2

Regular and Irregular Plurals

When we have more than one noun, we change singular words into plural forms.

Regular Plurals (Add -s / -es / -ves)

  • • book → books
  • • kite → kites
  • • shelf → shelves (f/fe changes to -ves)

Irregular Plurals (Completely Change!)

  • • tooth → teeth
  • • foot → feet
  • • person → people
  • • man → men | woman → women | child → children
  • • sheep → sheep (No change!)

🟢 Basic Level Exercises

Exercise 1 Topic: Greetings

Complete the greeting dialog: "How are you?" → "I'm ________! (great)"

Step-by-step Solution: The target response uses the adjective "great" after "I'm".
Answer: I'm great!
Exercise 2 Topic: Plurals (-s)

Write the plural form of the noun: kite → three ________

Step-by-step Solution: Add "-s" to regular nouns.
Answer: three kites
Exercise 3 Topic: Contractions

What does "We're" mean in full words?

Step-by-step Solution: "We're" combines the pronoun "We" and the verb "are".
Answer: We are
Exercise 4 Topic: Facial Expression

Complete: A boy with a big smile and bright eyes looks ________. (happy / angry)

Step-by-step Solution: A smile indicates happiness.
Answer: happy
Exercise 5 Topic: Plurals (-s)

Complete: I like to play with all my ________. (friend)

Step-by-step Solution: Make "friend" plural by adding -s.
Answer: friends

🔵 Intermediate Level Exercises

Exercise 6 Topic: Irregular Plurals

Complete with the correct plural of tooth: My rabbit has two big ________.

Step-by-step Solution: "Tooth" is an irregular noun; oo changes to ee.
Answer: teeth
Exercise 7 Topic: Irregular Plurals (f to ves)

Complete: There are books on the three ________ in my room. (shelf)

Step-by-step Solution: Nouns ending in -f change to -ves in plural.
Answer: shelves
Exercise 8 Topic: Expression (Look)

Look at the image description: A boy yawns and covers his mouth. Complete: He looks ________.

Step-by-step Solution: Yawning is a sign of tiredness.
Answer: tired
Exercise 9 Topic: Expression (Look)

Complete: A girl with a plate of spaghetti eating eagerly. Complete: She looks ________. (hungry / thirsty)

Step-by-step Solution: Eating food eagerly means she is hungry.

Exercise 10 Topic: Irregular Plurals

Complete: There are ten ________ on the train. (person)

🟣 Advanced Level Exercises

Exercise 11 Topic: Word Order

Unscramble the words to make a correct sentence: you? / How / are

Step-by-step Solution: Standard question order is Question Word (How) + Verb (are) + Pronoun (you?).
Answer: How are you?
Exercise 12 Topic: Combined Plurals

Correct the sentence error: "There are two man and three woman in the park."

Step-by-step Solution: Both man and woman are irregular and need plural forms (men, women).
Answer: "There are two men and three women in the park."
Exercise 13 Topic: Complex Expression

Fill in the blank with the correct form: "Look at Tom! He has a glass of water because he looks ________." (thirsty / hungry)

Step-by-step Solution: Drinking water relates to thirst.
Answer: thirsty
Exercise 14 Topic: Irregular Anatomy Plural

Find the error: "My dog ran fast with its four foots in the mud." Fix it.

Step-by-step Solution: The plural of foot is feet, not foots.
Answer: "My dog ran fast with its four feet in the mud."
Exercise 15 Topic: Invariable Plurals

Complete: In the playground picture, I can see five ________ grazing near the fence. (sheep)

Step-by-step Solution: Sheep is the same in singular and plural.
Answer: sheep

Critical Reflection & Logical Thinking 🧠

Discuss these questions with your teacher or classmates to deepen your understanding of English grammar in real life.

Question 1

Why do you think some words like tooth → teeth change completely in plural, while other words like kite → kites just add an -s?

Hint: Think about old historical languages and how English evolved over hundreds of years!
Question 2

How can observing a person's facial expressions (like looking hungry, angry, or excited) help us be better and more empathetic friends?

Hint: Empathy allows us to understand how others feel without them always needing to speak.
Question 3

When someone asks you "How are you?", why is it useful to have options beyond just saying "Fine"? How does saying "I'm tired" or "I'm great" change communication?

Hint: Specific feelings give accurate information about our energy and mood.
Question 4

Why doesn't the word sheep change when there are many of them? Can you think of other animals that stay the same? (Hint: fish!)

Hint: Certain farm and aquatic animals have invariable plural forms in English.
